DataSattva The clearer side of data

Putting the new website live — and getting the form results into Zoho Mail

A plain-language guide. No coding. You click through a few screens, copy a few values, and you're done. Read it once from start to finish before you begin, then follow it with the browser open.

What you are doing, in one paragraph Your website files are already built and sitting in a folder called dist. You will upload them to Vercel (the company that currently shows your site at www.datasattva.com), tell Vercel to show this new version instead of the old one, then connect a free Zoho sending service so that whenever somebody fills in the contact form or the assessment form, an email arrives in your Zoho Mail inbox.

The five parts

  1. Put the new website live — about 20 minutes
  2. Make sure Zoho Mail keeps working — 5 minutes of checking
  3. Connect the forms to Zoho (ZeptoMail) — about 25 minutes
  4. Test everything — 10 minutes
  5. If something goes wrong
  6. The values you need to type — one-page summary
Before you start, gather these Your Vercel login · your Namecheap login (password manager or 2FA phone handy) · your Zoho Mail login · the folder dist from this project · 30–40 minutes without interruptions. Do not start at 11 pm on a Friday. Do it in the morning on a weekday, so that if anything is off you can fix it the same day.
The one thing that would break your email Your domain datasattva.com is registered at Namecheap, and the records that tell the world "send this domain's email to Zoho" (called MX records) live at Namecheap. Somewhere in Vercel you will see an option offering to move your nameservers to Vercel. Say no. Moving nameservers moves those email records too, and Zoho Mail stops receiving. Everything in this guide works without touching nameservers.

Part 1 — Put the new website live

In plain words: your new site needs somewhere to live, and your address www.datasattva.com needs to point at the new site instead of the old one. Both are done inside Vercel.

About 20 minutes

Step 1.1 — Get the website files into a Vercel project

You have two ways. Pick one.

Way A — no GitHub (simplest)

  1. Install Vercel's command-line tool once. Open Terminal (Mac) or PowerShell (Windows) and type:
    npm i -g vercel
  2. In the same window, go into the project folder (the one containing dist) and type:
    vercel
  3. It asks you to log in — press Enter, it opens the browser, approve it.
  4. Accept the defaults it suggests (project name datasattva), and answer no to "Link to existing project?".
  5. When it finishes it prints a preview address ending in .vercel.app. Open it. That is your new site, live but not yet on your domain.

Way B — with GitHub

  1. Create an empty repository on GitHub (private is fine), push this project folder to it.
  2. In Vercel, click Add New… → Project.
  3. Choose Import Git Repository and pick the repository.
  4. Leave all settings as they are (the project already contains vercel.json, which tells Vercel what to do). Click Deploy.
  5. When it finishes, open the .vercel.app address it gives you.
Checkpoint The .vercel.app address should look exactly like your finished site: the navy header, the "Take the 3-Minute Assessment" button, and every page in the menu reachable. If it looks broken, stop and fix that first — do not move your domain onto it yet.

Step 1.2 — Add your domain to the new project

  1. Open the new project in Vercel Project → Settings → Domains.
  2. Type your domain In the box, type www.datasattva.com and click Add. Then do it again for datasattva.com (without the www).
  3. Accept the "move domain" prompt Because the domain already exists in your Vercel account (on the old project), Vercel will say something like "This domain is already in use by another project — move it here?". Click Move / Confirm.

    That single click is the actual switchover. Vercel changes where the domain points; Namecheap is not involved at all, and your email records are untouched.

  4. Set the main address Vercel will ask which one is the primary. Choose www.datasattva.com and let the version without www redirect to it.
  5. Wait for the tick Each domain line shows a status. It usually turns green within a minute or two. While it says "pending", just wait.
Checkpoint Open a new browser tab and go to https://www.datasattva.com. You should see the new site, with the little padlock in the address bar. Try three or four pages from the menu, and try it on your phone.

Step 1.3 — If you need to undo it

  1. In the old project: Settings → Domains → Add → www.datasattva.com → accept the move prompt again.
  2. Or, if the old site is the same project: Deployments → find the previous working deployment → ⋯ → Promote to Production.

Undoing takes about a minute, which is why doing this on a weekday morning is worth the effort.

Part 2 — Make sure Zoho Mail keeps working

In plain words: nothing you did in Part 1 touches email. This part is just five minutes of confirming that, so you notice a problem now rather than when a client's email bounces.

  1. Send yourself an email from outside From your phone (mobile data on, not office wi-fi) or a Gmail account, send a short email to your Zoho address, e.g. hello@datasattva.com. It should arrive within a minute.
  2. Reply from Zoho Mail Reply to that message. The reply should land in the other inbox. If both directions work, your MX, SPF and DKIM records are all still fine.
  3. Confirm you did not move nameservers In Vercel, open Settings → Domains. If you see a banner about "Vercel DNS" or nameservers, ignore it. In Namecheap, under Domain List → Manage → Nameservers, it should still read Namecheap BasicDNS (or your own custom nameservers) — not ns1.vercel-dns.com.
If step 1 fails and email stops arriving Go to Namecheap → Domain List → Manage → Advanced DNS. The Mail Settings should say Custom MX, and there should be three MX records pointing at mx.zoho.in (priority 10), mx2.zoho.in (20), mx3.zoho.in (50). If they are gone, Zoho's setup page (Zoho Mail Admin → Domains → your domain → Email Delivery / MX) shows the exact values to paste back in. Then wait 30 minutes and retest.

Part 3 — Connect the forms to Zoho Mail

In plain words: the website's forms cannot email you by themselves — they need a small sending service. Zoho has exactly that, called ZeptoMail. It is part of Zoho, it is free for 10,000 emails (plenty — your forms might send a few dozen a month), and the mail lands in the Zoho Mail inbox you already use.

About 25 minutes
Why not just use your Zoho Mail password in the website? Vercel deliberately blocks the old-fashioned way of sending mail (called SMTP) because it silently drops messages. Using ZeptoMail — Zoho's official sending service — is the reliable route, and it means your mailbox password is never stored inside the website. You will not type your Zoho password anywhere in this part.

Step 3.1 — Create the ZeptoMail account (5 min)

  1. Go to ZeptoMail Open zoho.com/zeptomail and click Sign up for free. Sign in with the same Zoho account you use for Zoho Mail — then everything lives in one place.
  2. Choose the India data centre if offered Zoho asks which region your account is in. Pick India (or the region your Zoho Mail already uses). Picking a different one from your mailbox is allowed, but keeping them the same is simpler.
  3. Create a "Mail Agent" ZeptoMail calls its sending identity a Mail Agent. Click Mail Agents → Add Mail Agent. Name it something obvious like Website forms.

Step 3.2 — Prove you own datasattva.com (10 min, mostly waiting)

  1. Add the domain Inside the Mail Agent, go to Domains (sometimes shown as "Sending Domains") → Add Domain → type datasattva.com → Add.

    Type it without www.

  2. Zoho shows you 2–3 DNS records They will look like a TXT record, a CNAME record, and possibly a second TXT for DKIM. Keep this browser tab open — you need to copy these values exactly.
  3. Paste them into Namecheap In a second tab: Namecheap → Domain List → Manage next to datasattva.com → Advanced DNS → Add New Record. For each record Zoho showed you, choose the matching Type, paste the Host and Value, leave TTL on Automatic, and click the green tick to save.
    Two small gotchas Namecheap adds the domain automatically, so if Zoho says host @ or datasattva.com, just enter @. And paste values with no spaces at the start or end — a stray space is the most common reason verification fails.
  4. Click Verify and wait Back in ZeptoMail, click Verify. It usually goes green in 5–30 minutes. If it still says pending after an hour, re-paste each record and check for typos.
The SPF record — the one place to be careful You already have an SPF record for Zoho Mail (a TXT record starting v=spf1 include:zoho.in …). There can only be one SPF record on a domain. If ZeptoMail asks you to add one and you already have it, do not create a second — edit the existing one so it includes both Zoho services. Zoho's own instructions show the exact combined line to use. Two SPF records cancel each other out and mail starts going to spam.

Step 3.3 — Copy the sending key (2 min)

  1. Open the Mail Agent's keys Mail Agent → SMTP/API (or Send Mail Token).
  2. Create a token Click Create Send Mail Token, name it Vercel website, and copy the long string it shows.
    Copy it now. ZeptoMail shows this key only once. If you lose it, delete the token and make a new one — no harm done, the old one simply stops working.

Step 3.4 — Put the key into Vercel (5 min)

  1. Open the environment variables screen Vercel → your project → Settings → Environment Variables.
  2. Add these five, one at a time For each: type the Key, paste the Value, tick all three environments (Production, Preview, Development), click Save.
    KeyValue to paste
    ZEPTOMAIL_SEND_TOKENthe long key you copied in step 3.3
    ZEPTOMAIL_API_HOSTapi.zeptomail.in  (India; use api.zeptomail.eu or api.zeptomail.com if you signed up in another region)
    ENQUIRY_TO_EMAILhello@datasattva.com  — the Zoho inbox that should receive form results
    ENQUIRY_FROM_EMAILDataSattva Website <website@datasattva.com>
    SITE_URLhttps://www.datasattva.com

    website@datasattva.com does not need to be a real mailbox you read — it only needs to be on the domain you verified. If you prefer, use hello@datasattva.com as the sender too. Add ZEPTOMAIL_BOUNCE_ADDRESS=bounce@datasattva.com later if you want bounce reports separated out.

  3. Redeploy — this step is not optional Vercel only reads new settings when a fresh deployment is built. Go to Deployments → the top row → ⋯ → Redeploy → confirm. Wait for "Ready".
Checkpoint Your project's Settings → Environment Variables should list all five keys. If the site will not build and the log mentions delivery not being configured, one of the keys is missing or was only saved for one environment — re-check, then redeploy.

Part 4 — Test everything

In plain words: fill in your own forms like a customer would, and confirm the email arrives. Ten minutes now saves a week of wondering whether enquiries are leaking.

  1. Send a contact enquiry On www.datasattva.com/contact, fill in the form with your own details, and in the message write "test — please ignore". Use a real email address you can check (a Gmail one is ideal, so you can also test replying). Submit.
  2. Check the page response You should see a success message containing a reference like DS-2026-XXXXXX. If instead you see a message asking you to email directly, delivery failed — see Part 5.
  3. Check Zoho Mail Within about a minute, the enquiry should be in the hello@datasattva.com inbox. Open it. Check three things: it is not in Spam, the layout is readable, and clicking Reply addresses your test email (not the website).
  4. Test the assessment form Open www.datasattva.com/dpdp-readiness-assessment, complete the questions, then use the "send me this result" box at the end with your own details. A second email should arrive, this one showing the score, band and the count of critical / high / moderate gaps.
  5. Test a mistake Submit the contact form with an empty name and an email typed as abc@. It should refuse and explain, without sending anything.
  6. Check mobile Do one submission from your phone. Half your visitors will be on a phone.
When all six pass You are live. Keep the ZeptoMail login and the token in your password manager, and note the date in your calendar for one year — that is when the free plan and the DNS records are worth re-checking.

Where to look for clues

Vercel keeps a log of every form submission attempt. Project → Logs (or Deployments → the deployment → Functions) and look for /api/enquiries. Each line says whether the email was delivered, and if it was not, the reason. Nothing sensitive is written to the page itself.
